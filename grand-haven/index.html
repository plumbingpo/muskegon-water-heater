Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Grand Haven

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away as soon as something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Grand Haven Homeowners Face

Whatever type of water heater your Grand Haven home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way.
