Keeping Your Roosevelt Park Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms such as discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
